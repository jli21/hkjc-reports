Top-choice movement

Not applicable to this model. Top-choice movement compares a model's first pick against the market's. This candidate is scored against its own comparator on fixed mean utilities, not against the market, so a market top-choice comparison would answer a different question than the rest of this report.

Ensemble stability

Not applicable to this model. There is no ensemble to be stable. This architecture is a choice *link* applied to one set of mean utilities, not a bag of independently fitted members, so seed variation here is integration noise rather than model disagreement -- and that is reported in the appendix instead.

Feature and correction effects

Not applicable to this model. There are no per-feature effects to attribute. The link changes how utilities become probabilities; the utilities themselves come from the comparator's fit, whose feature effects are reported in that model's own report.

Representative races

Not applicable to this model. Representative races are chosen by how much a model's correction moved a runner's probability. This candidate applies a link to fixed mean utilities, so the movement to explain belongs to the comparator's fit and is shown in that model's report. A race chosen here would be chosen by integration noise.

Betting and bankroll analysis

Not applicable to this model. No bet ledger has been settled for Probit Offset with a pace factor. The probability comparison stands on its own; staking a candidate that has not passed its promotion gates would invite reading a bankroll path as a recommendation.

What a simulation cannot tell you

The model-assumed simulation draws winners from the model's own probabilities. If those probabilities are wrong the simulation is confidently wrong in the same direction, so it bounds sampling risk and says nothing about model risk.

The fitted scale sits above the value theory predicts, in every year: 1.993 to 2.228 against 0.780 (the scale the fallback year used, which is the predeclared theoretical value). Theory gets this wrong and the fit is what the report uses -- a scale asserted from theory would divide the utilities by too little, and at that value the market-relative candidate loses instead of winning. A run that took the theoretical value on faith would have reported the wrong sign, which is why the first year -- which has no history and falls back to it -- is excluded from the headline and labelled.

Limitations and the retrospective-odds warning

Retrospective odds, restated

Every stake, return and bankroll figure in this report is computed against historical result-page odds and official dividends. Those are not an executable pre-race price: they are the prices after the pool closed, and a bet placed at them could not have been placed. Nothing here establishes profitability. A profitability claim requires a locked model shadow-tested against timestamped pre-race odds snapshots.

Restated at the end as well as beside the betting numbers, deliberately. It is the single most load-bearing caveat in this report and the one a reader skimming for a return figure is most likely to miss.

Every test year is also development data

Every year through 2026 has been used as development data in this repository: features were searched, architectures compared and thresholds inspected against these same seasons. The chronological split is real, so a test year is genuinely after its training rows, but no test year is unseen. Treat the forward figures as probability progress rather than as a locked out-of-sample result, and do not select a Kelly level or an edge threshold from this reused history. The confirmation step that this run cannot substitute for is a prospective shadow test against archived pre-race price snapshots.

What else this report does not establish

  • It measures Probit Offset with a pace factor against the incumbent Offset model on the same margins. Beating a comparator and beating the market are different claims.
  • Its intervals condition on the fitted model, so they bound sampling risk and not model risk.
  • Every figure is out of sample in time but in sample in design: the feature set, the hyperparameters and the staking rules were all chosen with these years visible.
  • Nothing here is a shadow test. A profitability claim needs a locked model run forward against timestamped pre-race prices.
