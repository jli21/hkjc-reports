Understanding the HKJC models

A mathematical reading guide · 20 September 2026

This guide explains the models, their assumptions and their relationship to betting. It distinguishes established implementation facts, historical findings and proposed research. The decision benchmark for this project discussion is 10% Kelly with rebate-aware sizing and settlement. Other Kelly fractions are sensitivity checks, not substitutes for that benchmark.

1. The three problems we are solving

Prediction asks which horse wins and with what probability. Estimation asks how to learn those probabilities from limited historical data. Decision-making asks which bets to place at the available prices, under the bankroll and rebate rules. A model can improve one of these without improving all three.

Let race r contain n runners. Let y be its winner, x_i the information about runner i, d_i its gross decimal payout multiple, q_i its normalized market probability, c_i a learned correction, and p_i its model winning probability. The race probabilities must be nonnegative and sum to one.

Normalized q is a probability benchmark. It is not the executable payout: normalizing removes the market overround, whereas the bettor still receives d. Winning probabilities alone also do not predict future dividend movement.

Each race contributes once. Treating its twelve runners as twelve independent outcomes understates uncertainty. Historical comparisons should pair the same complete fields; meeting-level resampling also allows races on the same day to move together.

Field-aware features can already change the utilities, so the restriction is conditional on the scores, not a statement that the entire pipeline ignores the field. Iid Probit generally lacks this fixed-ratio property even though its latent errors are independent.

5. Why our Probit offset needs an anchor

Softmax of log(q) returns q. Probit of log(q) generally does not. Replacing the link naively therefore changes the market baseline even if the learned correction is zero.

The project avoids that with a ratio anchor:

At c=0, a and b coincide and the model returns q exactly apart from numerical precision. This is a market-preserving transformation of the correction, not an unmodified structural model of race performance.

The scale divides utility differences inside the integrator. Larger scale generally weakens the influence of utility gaps; in the large-scale limit the anchored correction vanishes. This is not identical to multiplying only c by a constant, because the market utilities enter both integrations.

6. Link-only versus native Probit boosting

Link-only Probit reuses utilities learned under Softmax and changes their probability mapping. Native Probit boosting instead grows trees against:

Why can the seemingly more aligned approach lose? Historical loss is a noisy estimate of future loss. The objective changes gradients, splits, effective regularization and confidence. The implementation also uses an approximate diagonal curvature treatment. Our native booster trains at a fixed scale, then fits a forward scoring scale: it is not jointly optimizing the final evaluated pipeline at every step.

The code records an earlier failure in which fitting scale on training data increasingly sharpened overfit corrections. A better fit to those labels worsened future performance. Separating utility learning from low-dimensional forward calibration can therefore be beneficial regularization.

The historical controlled research found that changing the link helped, while native Probit training gave back much of the gain. This supports the tested link-only procedure; it does not prove that every possible Probit training algorithm must lose.

7. Bagging: what is actually averaged?

The existing multi-seed model fits several members using different randomness, then averages final probability vectors. In this project, calling this bagging does not imply that every member was trained on an independently bootstrapped sample of races. Shared data and similar learners make their errors correlated.

Averaging normalized vectors preserves their sum. Small numerical renormalization may still be applied. Average probabilities are a mixture: choose a member at random, then draw from that member's predicted race distribution.

Independent noise decreases with M; shared error remains. That is why ten very similar members can contribute little beyond five. Seeds are not independent racing seasons and must not be treated as independent observations for confidence intervals.

The ensemble's loss cannot exceed the average member loss on that same outcome. This does not guarantee it beats the best member, another model or the market.

Averaging utilities is different. For Softmax, averaging utilities produces a normalized geometric combination of member probabilities, whereas averaging probabilities produces an arithmetic mixture:

For a two-runner race, members assigning A probabilities 0.9 and 0.5 give an arithmetic average of 0.7. Their average logits are log(9)/2 and produce probability 0.75. These are different models.

QuerySoftMax's current saved run has one seed. It has no five-member disagreement to shrink. Constructing such an ensemble requires additional declared fits; it cannot be manufactured by copying the same predictions.

9. Other model families in context

The older linear models constrain corrections to additive feature effects. Market-covariate versions learn coefficients on odds-related inputs; an offset fixes a baseline coefficient structurally. These are different assumptions.

Plackett–Luce extends the Softmax construction along a finishing order pi:

The earlier mixed-effects approach added regularized horse, jockey and trainer effects. Full finishing orders offer more observations about relative performance, but learning the whole order can emphasize distinctions less relevant to winning or to betting.

The historical GRU/field-attention architecture changes representation: a recurrent network summarizes prior starts, and attention conditions runner representations on the field. More expressive interactions can help only if sufficient trustworthy data and regularization support their estimation.

Gu-style stochastic approximation concerns how latent Gaussian utility parameters are estimated, including winner-conditioned sampling. It is not synonymous with arbitrary correlated-error Probit. Its imported-feature workflow is deferred; comparisons involving the older inputs are not clean tests against current native-feature models.

Why survival is not proof of an edge

A model can repeatedly choose negative-expectation bets and survive a finite history. Without rebates, a losing bet staking 10% of remaining capital leaves 90%. Twenty consecutive losses leave:

It has lost almost 88% without reaching literal zero. Ten percent Kelly usually stakes much less than 10% of capital per bet; these two phrases must not be confused. Lower exposure, rebates and occasional large winners can all delay or reverse realized losses. Survival is evidence about one realized path, not a statistical proof of positive expectation. Practical ruin can also occur well above zero.

QuerySoftMax's different selections may still be useful. The appropriate question is whether the disagreements retain favorable outcomes and rebates out of time, with tolerable drawdowns and without dependence on a few meetings.

11. Reading feature reports and experiment evidence

Tree feature importance describes a fitted utility learner. It is not a causal effect and is not automatically a probability-space effect after a new link. A link-only model inherits the source trees; it does not refit feature relationships. If source importance is shown, label it as inherited utility importance.

Log loss, calibration, top-choice accuracy, market correlation and wealth measure different properties. A model can improve average loss yet worsen decisions near betting thresholds. Conversely a profitable historical subset does not establish accurate probabilities throughout the field.

Keep complete race populations, training cutoffs, features, source hashes and comparison weights visible. Never average seed-level loss and call it ensemble loss. Never average annual means and call it a pooled race mean. Confidence intervals over previously researched years do not include the full uncertainty from having tried many experiments.

Change the probability mapping, fit scale on prior out-of-time years only, retain the warm-up flag, and compare against both raw QuerySoftMax and the existing Probit offset. Do not promote automatically. Report features, calibration, settlement and simulation, with 10% rebate-aware Kelly as the decision headline.

The saved positive Softmax probabilities identify the source utilities up to a race-specific constant: log(p_i) = u_i - log(sum exp(u_j)). Probit is invariant to that common shift, so log probabilities can recover the exact utility differences required by the new link. This is valid only for the uncalibrated, unblended single-member source; taking logs of a calibrated ensemble would not recover its members' original utilities.
